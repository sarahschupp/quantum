April 2026: Bottom Line

April 2026 was the strongest month of the engagement: 326,360 impressions, 30,990 clicks, and a record 10.34% ER across 66 posts, all with zero paid media spend. Clicks rose +14.4% vs. March while impressions held essentially flat (-2.8%).

Standout signal: HPC-tagged content averaged 24.9% ER, directly mapping to QuEra's buyer persona for quantum-HPC integration. Founder/exec content dominated top impressions. The QEC pre-print (Apr 20) reached 30.5% ER, the highest click-through rate of the month.

Reading the Numbers
Impressions were essentially flat
LinkedIn native: 326,360 in April vs. 335,709 in March = -2.8%. Month-to-month impression parity at this volume and post count (66 vs. 66) indicates a stable channel. The larger gap shown in prior Buffer data was a reporting artifact.
Clicks and ER both improved
Clicks rose 14.4% (30,990 vs. 27,080) and ER reached 10.34%, the highest single month in the six-month engagement, above January's previous high of 9.89%.

Watch Items
April Observations Worth Flagging
HPC integration posts punch above their impression weight
Posts tagged #HPC generated an average 24.9% ER across three posts in April, unusually high for a non-document format. That audience (HPC decision-makers and engineers) clicks through at a much higher rate than the general LinkedIn audience, and it is exactly the buyer persona QuEra is targeting for near-term commercial deployments. The HPC User Forum (May 5 to 6, Austin) is the primary May event to build on this audience signal. This directly maps to the buyer persona for QuEra's quantum-HPC integration positioning.
Founder and exec content is now the clearest impression driver
Three of the top five impressions posts featured a named founder or executive (Lukin, Gemelke, Boger). Posts attributed to a named expert consistently outperform unattributed research summaries on impressions, a pattern that has held across all six months. More of these, particularly the interview and op-ed format, would directly move the top-line numbers.
Comments declined 43% vs. March (56 vs. 98)
March's comment spike was driven by the APS hockey match post and Bell Prize reaction. April had no equivalent high-velocity post. The underlying rate (roughly 0.85 per post) is above the Jan/Feb baseline. This is not a structural decline: it reflects the absence of a marquee milestone post in April. Both months' figures are from LinkedIn native analytics.
